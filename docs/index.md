# How Coimbra Shaped West-East Connections

Notes on the relationship between Coimbra, Portugal, and China, focusing on Jesuit missions.

Coimbra was the site of the first Jesuit college, established in 1543. It was created with the specific purpose of training future missionaries to the East.

With an endowment for 200 students, it was to be the primary recruitment center for missionaries destined for China.

Since 1537, King John III of Portugal was developing in Coimbra a university intended to be one of the best in Europe. Mobilizing significant financial resources and pressing bishops and religious orders to join the project, the king transformed the city, building new colleges and repurposing the former royal palace as the center of academic life. Some of the best professors were hired for Coimbra, and later a preparatory school was created to prepare future university students.

The king's goal? To produce the much-needed human resources for his expanding overseas possessions and to staff the many instances where matters of faith and church were crucial for the fostering of Portugal's interests in the global stage.

This goal intersected with the path of a small group of graduates of the University of Paris who in 1534 had sworn to defend the christian faith and obediance to the Pope, creating the seed of what would quickly become one the most remarkable global organizations in history: the Jesuits.
